## Suggested Custom Diagrams for Attenborough × Feynman Voice

Beyond the standard anatomical figures, these diagrams would enhance understanding:

### 1. Localization Map: Five Regions of the Neurological Exam
A coronal (front-facing) view of the brain and brainstem with five distinct regions color-coded:
- **Cerebral cortex (blue)**: Mental status exam location
- **Brainstem with labeled nuclei (red)**: Cranial nerve exam location
  - Midbrain: oculomotor nucleus
  - Pons: abducens nucleus, vestibular nuclei
  - Medulla: hypoglossal nucleus, vagal nucleus
- **Spinal cord (orange)**: Motor and sensory exam location
- **Cerebellum (green)**: Coordination exam location
- **Peripheral nerves (yellow)**: Spinal and cranial nerves

**Purpose:** Shows at a glance how each exam section corresponds to a specific anatomical region.

### 2. Cross-Section Comparison: UMN vs. LMN Lesion
Two side-by-side coronal views of motor pathways:
- **Left panel (UMN lesion)**: Motor cortex or corticospinal tract damage
  - Shows intact lower motor neurons
  - Annotates: spasticity, hyperreflexia, positive Babinski
- **Right panel (LMN lesion)**: Spinal cord ventral horn or peripheral nerve damage
  - Shows damaged lower motor neurons
  - Annotates: flaccidity, areflexia, negative Babinski, fasciculation
- **Arrows** show flow of damage down the motor pathway

**Purpose:** Directly contrasts the two major categories of motor disease and their signature findings.

### 3. Brainstem Lesion Localization Chart
Horizontal slices through midbrain, pons, and medulla showing:
- Location of major nuclei: oculomotor, trochlear, abducens, trigeminal, facial, vestibulocochlear, glossopharyngeal, vagus, hypoglossal, accessory
- Corticospinal tract pathway and its crossing point in medulla
- Medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF)
- Spinothalamic and dorsal column pathways
- For each nucleus, note what deficits result from damage

**Purpose:** Clarifies why brainstem lesions affect multiple functions; shows how adjacent nuclei and fiber tracts are at risk from small lesions.

### 4. Spinal Cord Hemisection: Pattern of Deficits
Diagram of a spinal cord cross-section with one half damaged (right side):
- **On the injured side, below the lesion:**
  - Motor paralysis (corticospinal tract damaged ipsilaterally)
  - Loss of fine touch and proprioception (dorsal column damaged ipsilaterally, hasn't crossed yet)
  - Preserved pain sensation (spinothalamic pathway already crossed, ascends on the intact left side)
- **On the intact side, below the lesion:**
  - Preserved motor function
  - Preserved fine touch and proprioception (dorsal column intact)
  - Loss of pain sensation (spinothalamic fibers crossed at lesion level, now ascending through damaged right side)

**Purpose:** Shows how understanding pathway crossing and anatomy allows localization from the pattern of sensory loss alone.

### 5. Cranial Nerve Tour Through the Brainstem
A sagittal (side-view) illustration of the brainstem with:
- Brainstem levels labeled (midbrain, pons, medulla)
- Each cranial nerve emerging from its level
- A color code linking each nerve to its primary function
- For nuclei with multiple functions (e.g., vagus), list the functions

**Purpose:** Reinforces the concept that the cranial nerve exam is a tour through brainstem anatomy.

### 6. Cerebellar Circuit: The Comparator Model
A block diagram showing:
- Cerebral cortex motor command → Pons → Middle cerebellar peduncle
- Sensory feedback (proprioception, vestibular, visual) → Inferior cerebellar peduncle
- Cerebellar cortex compares command with feedback
- Output → Superior cerebellar peduncle → Red nucleus → Spinal cord
- **Annotation:** What happens when each stage is damaged (proprioceptive ataxia, vestibular ataxia, cerebellar ataxia)

**Purpose:** Illustrates the functional principle of the cerebellum and explains how different causes produce ataxia.

## Diagram Integration Notes

- Figures should appear near the text that references them, with clear captions
- Each figure should have a caption that explains what to notice in the diagram and why it matters for understanding the exam
- Refer to figures by name, not by number (e.g., "Figure: Cerebellar Circuit" not "Fig. 5")
- Interactive versions (clickable labels, animations) would enhance understanding but are optional for print version
