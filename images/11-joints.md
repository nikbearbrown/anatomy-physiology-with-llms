# Chapter 11: Joints — Images and Figures

## Figures needed for chapter

### 1. Structural Classification of Joints
**Purpose:** Show the three structural types side by side with cross-sections and examples.

**Elements:**
- Fibrous joint (suture): tight fibrous tissue between bone surfaces, no joint cavity
- Cartilaginous joint (intervertebral disc): bones separated by cartilage, limited space
- Synovial joint (knee): bones separated by joint cavity, synovial fluid visible

**Labels:** fibrous connective tissue, joint cavity, synovial fluid, articular cartilage, hyaline cartilage, fibrocartilage

**Variants by complexity:**
- Simple: three columns labeled with examples
- Detailed: cross-sections with depth showing how tissues are arranged
- Pedagogical: the same three side-by-side, with an arrow showing the stability-mobility spectrum

---

### 2. Functional Classification: Synarthrosis, Amphiarthrosis, Diarthrosis
**Purpose:** Show examples of each functional class with the joints they represent.

**Elements:**
- Synarthrosis: skull suture (no space, tightly bound)
- Amphiarthrosis: intervertebral disc (small space, cartilaginous connection, some movement allowed)
- Diarthrosis: elbow or knee (large joint cavity, free movement)

**Note:** Could be organized as a progression showing increasing joint cavity size and increasing freedom of movement.

---

### 3. Synovial Joint Architecture — Detailed Cross-Section
**Purpose:** Label all components of a synovial joint: capsule, synovial membrane, synovial fluid, articular cartilage, ligaments, bone, meniscus (if applicable).

**Model joint:** Use the knee as the example (it has most of the structures) or a simplified generalized synovial joint.

**Labels to show:**
- Articulating bones
- Articular cartilage (thin, smooth layer on bone surface)
- Joint cavity
- Synovial fluid (shown as shading within the cavity)
- Synovial membrane (inner lining of capsule)
- Fibrous capsule (outer layer)
- Ligaments (extrinsic, outside the capsule)
- Meniscus or articular disc (if showing knee, show the C-shaped menisci)
- Bursa (if space allows, show a small bursa nearby)

### 4. Six Synovial Joint Types
**Purpose:** Show the shape of each articulating surface and give an example of each.

**Organized as a table or 2×3 grid:**

| Type | Shape | Example | Movement |
|------|-------|---------|----------|
| Hinge | convex-concave | Elbow | flexion/extension only |
| Pivot | rounded in ring | Atlantoaxial (C1-C2) | rotation only |
| Condyloid | shallow depression + rounded | Knuckle (MCP) joint | flexion/extension, abduction/adduction |
| Saddle | concave-convex both ways | Thumb base (CMC) | flexion/extension, abduction/adduction |
| Ball-and-socket | sphere in depression | Hip, shoulder | all movements (multiaxial) |
| Plane | flat surfaces | Vertebral facet joints | small sliding motions |

**Drawings:** Each should show the articulating surfaces in enough detail to see the shape. Arrows could indicate allowed movement(s).

### 5. Movement Vocabulary — Axial and Non-Axial Movements
**Purpose:** Show examples of each movement type with a standing or anatomical position figure as reference.

**Movements to illustrate:**
- Flexion/extension (at elbow, knee, hip, neck)
- Abduction/adduction (at shoulder, hip, fingers)
- Rotation (at shoulder, hip, cervical spine)
- Circumduction (at shoulder, hip)
- Pronation/supination (forearm)
- Dorsiflexion/plantarflexion (ankle)
- Inversion/eversion (foot)
- Opposition (thumb)

**Best formatted as:** A central figure (front and back views, standing position) with arrows showing each movement. Could be color-coded by joint.

### 6. The Shoulder Joint (Glenohumeral Joint)
**Purpose:** Show the anatomy of the shoulder, emphasizing the shallow socket and loose ligament support. Include the rotator cuff.

**Elements:**
- Humerus and humeral head (the "ball")
- Scapula and glenoid cavity (the shallow "socket")
- Glenoid labrum (fibrocartilage rim)
- Articular capsule
- Intrinsic ligaments (glenohumeral ligaments)
- Rotator cuff muscles and tendons (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis)
- Bursa (subacromial bursa)

**Key feature:** The shallow socket should be obviously shallow compared to the hip socket in a parallel figure.

**Variants:** Could show anterior and posterior views, or anterior view with deep and superficial layers.

---

### 7. The Hip Joint — Comparison with Shoulder
**Purpose:** Contrast with the shoulder joint, showing the deep socket and strong ligaments.

**Elements:**
- Femur and femoral head (the "ball")
- Pelvis and acetabulum (the deep "socket")
- Acetabular labrum (fibrocartilage rim)
- Hip ligaments (iliofemoral, pubofemoral, ischiofemoral, ligamentum teres)
- Joint capsule
- Stronger, thicker structure overall

**Key feature:** The deep socket and tight ligament arrangement should emphasize stability over mobility.

**Parallel to shoulder image:** Could be shown side-by-side with the shoulder for direct comparison.

---

### 8. The Knee Joint — Complex Multi-Articulation
**Purpose:** Show the three articulations (femoropatellar, medial tibiofemoral, lateral tibiofemoral) and support structures.

**Elements:**
- Femur (thighbone) with condyles
- Tibia (shinbone) with condyles
- Patella (kneecap) and patellar groove
- Medial and lateral menisci (C-shaped fibrocartilage pads)
- Articular capsule
- Intrinsic ligaments (cruciate ligaments: ACL, PCL)
- Extrinsic ligaments (tibial and fibular collateral ligaments)
- Patellar ligament (tendon from quadriceps)

**Variants:** Anterior view (showing collateral ligaments), posterior view (showing cruciate ligaments), cross-section view (showing menisci), or exploded view showing multiple structures.

**Key learning points:** The menisci act as shock absorbers and load distributors; the cruciate ligaments resist hyperextension and excessive rotation; the complexity explains why knee injuries are so common.

---

### 9. The Ankle and Foot Joints
**Purpose:** Show the talocrural joint (ankle hinge) and the plane joints of the foot (intertarsal joints).

**Elements:**
- Tibia and fibula (end of the leg bones)
- Talus bone
- Medial malleolus (inner ankle bone)
- Lateral malleolus (outer ankle bone)
- Medial ligaments (deltoid ligament)
- Lateral ligaments (anterior talofibular, posterior talofibular, calcaneofibular)
- Tarsal bones (calcaneus, navicular, cuboid)
- Intertarsal joints

**Key features:** The ankle is a uniaxial hinge joint; the foot has multiple plane joints that allow inversion and eversion.

---

### 10. The Vertebral Column — Amphiarthrodial Joints
**Purpose:** Show how multiple small movements at intervertebral discs and facet joints allow overall spinal flexibility.

**Elements:**
- Three vertebrae (cervical, thoracic, lumbar examples)
- Intervertebral discs between bodies
- Vertebral bodies
- Facet joints (zygapophysial joints) between articular processes
- Spinal cord (shown in cross-section)
- Ligaments (interspinous, anterior longitudinal, posterior longitudinal)

**Key learning:** Show how the small movements (a few degrees at each disc) add up to large overall movements (full spinal flexion, extension, rotation).
