### Part One: The Dual-Circuit Pump

**[FIGURE: Dual circuits side-by-side]**
- Left side: right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary trunk → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body
- Right side: body tissues → superior/inferior venae cavae → right atrium
- Pressure labels: left ventricle 120 mmHg systolic, right ventricle 25 mmHg systolic

### Part Two: The Conduction System

**[FIGURE: Conduction pathway on the heart surface]**
- SA node location: right atrial wall near superior vena cava entry
- Internodal pathways: three bands (anterior, middle, posterior) from SA to AV node
- Bachmann's bundle: interatrial pathway
- AV node location: inferior right atrium in atrioventricular septum
- Bundle of His: penetrates interventricular septum
- Left and right bundle branches: descend both sides of septum
- Purkinje fibers: terminate at apex and spread upward
- Timeline overlay: SA node fires (0 ms) → atrial depolarization complete (50 ms) → AV node pause (100 ms) → ventricular depolarization complete (225 ms)
- Purpose: shows the hierarchical conduction system and timing

**[FIGURE: ECG with conduction overlaid]**
- ECG trace: P wave (atrial), PR interval (AV delay), QRS complex (ventricular), T wave (repolarization)
- Conduction events aligned: P wave = atrial depolarization, PR interval spans AV delay, QRS complex = ventricular depolarization
- Purpose: connects electrical signal (ECG) to mechanical events (conduction pathway)

### Part Three: Pressure-Volume Loop and Cardiac Cycle

**[FIGURE: Pressure-volume loop, labeled phases]**
- X-axis: volume (mL), 50–150 mL range
- Y-axis: pressure (mmHg), 0–140 mmHg range
- Bottom-left (filling): volume ~130 mL, pressure ~0–10 mmHg, low pressure fills from atrium
- Isovolumic contraction: vertical line from (130, 0) to (130, ~120), pressure rises with constant volume
- Ventricular ejection: rightward downward curve from (130, 120) to (60, ~100), pressure falls as volume decreases
- Isovolumic relaxation: vertical line from (60, 100) to (60, ~0), pressure falls with constant volume
- Filling again: rightward upward curve from (60, 0) to (130, 0), volume increases as pressure remains low
- Area enclosed: represents mechanical work
- Normal range annotations: EDV 120–130, ESV 50–60
- Purpose: encodes the entire cardiac cycle in one diagram; diagnostic tool for dysfunction

**[FIGURE: Valves and blood flow during each phase]**
- Atrial systole: AV valves open (tricuspid, mitral), semilunar valves closed (pulmonary, aortic)
- Isovolumic contraction: all valves closed, pressure rising
- Ventricular ejection: AV valves closed, semilunar valves open, blood flowing out
- Isovolumic relaxation: all valves closed, pressure falling
- Passive filling: AV valves open, semilunar valves closed, blood flowing in
- Purpose: shows which valves are open/closed and why at each phase

**[FIGURE: Heart sounds with valve closure events]**
- Timeline: atrial systole → ventricular systole (isovolumic) → ventricular ejection → ventricular relaxation (isovolumic) → filling
- S1 ("lub"): marked at AV valve closure (onset of ventricular contraction), turbulent flow as pressure reverses
- S2 ("dub"): marked at semilunar valve closure (onset of ventricular relaxation), turbulent flow as aorta and pulmonary artery pressures push back
- Purpose: connects sound to mechanical event (pressure reversal, turbulence) not just valve closure

**[FIGURE: Preload, afterload, contractility diagram]**
- Preload illustration: muscle at rest vs. stretched, force generated increases with stretch
- Afterload illustration: light weight (low resistance) vs. heavy weight (high resistance), more work required
- Contractility illustration: same preload/afterload, but stronger contraction (sympathetic activation)
- Purpose: shows the three variables that govern stroke volume

### Part Four: Autonomic Regulation

**[FIGURE: Medullary cardiovascular centers and autonomic innervation]**
- Medulla oblongata: cardioaccelerator center (sympathetic) and cardioinhibitory center (parasympathetic)
- Sympathetic pathway: spinal cord → cardiac nerves → SA and AV nodes and myocardium
- Parasympathetic pathway: vagus nerve (CN X) → parasympathetic fibers to SA and AV nodes
- Effect overlays: sympathetic = increase HR/contractility, parasympathetic = decrease HR
- Purpose: shows the dual control of heart rate

## Sizing and visual strategy

All figures should be simple, clean, high-contrast. Avoid 3D-rendered photo-realistic images (they distract from the mechanism). Use:
- Schematic diagrams with minimal decoration
- Anatomical outlines with key structures labeled
- Line graphs (pressure-volume loop, ECG) with gridlines
- Color coding: red for oxygenated/systemic, blue for deoxygenated/pulmonary
- Callouts for key pressures (120 mmHg, 25 mmHg, etc.)

## Accessibility notes

- All figures include text descriptions of key relationships (not just visual)
- Pressure-volume loop uses both visual position (location on graph) and text labels (EDV, ESV, stroke volume)
- Color is not the only distinguishing feature (also line style, labels, annotations)
