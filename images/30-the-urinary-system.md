# Images and Figures: The Urinary System

## Figures Referenced in Chapter

### Figure 1: Glomerulus and Bowman's Capsule
**Type:** Microscopy (electron micrograph) + diagram
**Purpose:** Show the filtration membrane in detail
**Key labels:**
- Afferent arteriole (bringing blood in)
- Efferent arteriole (carrying blood away)
- Glomerular capillary
- Bowman's capsule
- Podocytes (with pedicels)
- Fenestrations in capillary endothelial cells
- Filtration slits between pedicels
- Basement membrane

**Caption for chapter:** "The glomerulus is a high-pressure capillary bed surrounded by Bowman's capsule. Podocytes extend finger-like pedicels that lock together to form filtration slits. Fenestrations (70 nm pores) in the capillary wall, combined with the basement membrane and filtration slits, create a barrier that excludes cells and large proteins but allows ions, water, and small molecules to pass."

### Figure 2: Nephron Anatomy (Full Overview)
**Type:** Diagram (cross-sectional view of kidney + enlarged nephron)
**Purpose:** Show all regions of the nephron and their locations
**Key labels:**
- Glomerulus and Bowman's capsule (in cortex)
- Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) coiling in cortex
- Loop of Henle (descending and ascending limbs in medulla)
- Distal convoluted tubule (DCT) in cortex
- Collecting duct (in medulla)
- Vasa recta (capillary network)
- Peritubular capillaries
- Renal artery and vein
- Ureter

**Caption for chapter:** "The nephron is a specialized tubule with five main regions: the renal corpuscle (glomerulus + Bowman's capsule) in the cortex, the proximal tubule where heavy reabsorption occurs, the loop of Henle in the medulla where osmotic gradients are created, the distal tubule where fine-tuning occurs, and the collecting duct where water recovery is hormonally regulated. Blood capillaries (vasa recta) parallel the loop of Henle and collecting duct to recover solutes and water."

### Figure 3: Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA)
**Type:** Diagram (zoomed-in view of DCT and arterioles)
**Purpose:** Show the anatomical relationship between macula densa and juxtaglomerular cells
**Key labels:**
- Afferent arteriole
- Efferent arteriole
- Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)
- Macula densa (cluster of cuboidal cells in DCT)
- Juxtaglomerular cells (in afferent arteriole wall)
- Single cilium on macula densa

**Caption for chapter:** "The juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA) is a sensory complex where macula densa cells in the DCT detect sodium concentration and fluid flow, and juxtaglomerular cells in the afferent arteriole wall release renin in response. This sensing system initiates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone cascade to regulate blood pressure."

### Figure 4: Net Filtration Pressure (NFP) Diagram
**Type:** Diagram with pressure values (bar graph or side-by-side comparison)
**Purpose:** Illustrate the balance of forces that determine filtration rate
**Key elements:**
- Glomerular blood hydrostatic pressure: 55 mm Hg (arrow pushing filtrate out)
- Capsular hydrostatic pressure: 15 mm Hg (arrow pushing filtrate back)
- Blood colloid osmotic pressure: 30 mm Hg (arrow pulling water back)
- Net result: 10 mm Hg (the effective filtration pressure)

**Caption for chapter:** "Net filtration pressure (NFP) is the balance of forces across the glomerular membrane. Hydrostatic pressure in the capillary (55 mm Hg) pushes fluid out, while hydrostatic pressure in Bowman's capsule (15 mm Hg) and colloid osmotic pressure in the blood (30 mm Hg) push/pull fluid back. The net pressure (10 mm Hg) is small—only 2 percent of glomerular pressure—making the system fragile to changes in blood pressure or plasma protein levels."

### Figure 5: Reabsorption in the Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)
**Type:** Diagram (cross-section of tubule + magnified view of cell with transport proteins)
**Purpose:** Show the mechanisms of glucose, ion, and water reabsorption in the PCT
**Key labels:**
- Brush border (microvilli on apical surface)
- Lumen of PCT
- Apical membrane with Na+/glucose symporters
- Basal membrane with Na+/K+ ATPase
- Mitochondria (providing ATP)
- Peritubular capillary
- Na+ gradient created by ATPase

**Caption for chapter:** "In the proximal tubule, glucose is reabsorbed via Na+/glucose symporters on the apical membrane. Sodium drives glucose into the cell against its concentration gradient. The Na+/K+ ATPase on the basal side pumps sodium out, maintaining the electrochemical gradient. Glucose exits the basal side by facilitated diffusion into the peritubular capillary. Water follows osmotically (obligatory water reabsorption)."

### Figure 6: Loop of Henle and Countercurrent Multiplier System
**Type:** Diagram (cross-sectional view of loop + osmolarity gradient shown with color or numbers)
**Purpose:** Show osmolarity changes in descending vs. ascending limbs and the role of vasa recta
**Key features:**
- Descending limb: simple squamous epithelium, aquaporin channels, water leaves
- Osmolarity gradient: 300 mOsmol/kg at top → 1,200 mOsmol/kg at bottom
- Ascending limb: cuboidal epithelium, no aquaporin, Na+ pumped out, osmolarity drops to 100 mOsmol/kg
- Vasa recta: runs parallel, slowly picks up and releases solutes and water
- Arrows showing fluid flow (countercurrent) and osmotic gradients

**Caption for chapter:** "The loop of Henle creates a countercurrent multiplier system. The descending limb is permeable to water; water osmotically leaves as osmolarity in the surrounding medulla increases. The ascending limb is impermeable to water but actively pumps Na+ and Cl− out, diluting the filtrate to 100 mOsmol/kg. This differential permeability, combined with salt pumping, creates and maintains a salt gradient (reaching 1,200 mOsmol/kg deep in the medulla). The vasa recta capillaries preserve this gradient by slowly moving through the medulla, allowing sodium to enter and water to leave on the way down, then sodium to leave and water to enter on the way back up."

### Figure 7: Collecting Duct and ADH Regulation
**Type:** Diagram (cell cross-section with aquaporin channels)
**Purpose:** Show how ADH controls water reabsorption via aquaporin insertion
**Key elements:**
- Principal cell of collecting duct
- Apical membrane (facing lumen)
- Basal membrane (facing interstitial space)
- Aquaporin water channels (shown as present or absent based on ADH)
- ADH receptor on cell surface
- Vesicles containing aquaporin channels
- With ADH: channels inserted, water reabsorbed
- Without ADH: channels absent, water excreted

**Caption for chapter:** "In the collecting duct, ADH regulates water recovery by controlling aquaporin channel insertion. When ADH is present (high blood osmolarity), it binds to receptors on principal cells, triggering the fusion of vesicles containing aquaporins with the apical membrane. Water is then osmotically pulled from the collecting duct into the surrounding medullary interstitium and into the vasa recta. Without ADH, these channels are not inserted, and water remains in the filtrate, producing dilute urine."

### Figure 8: Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)
**Type:** Flowchart (cascade diagram)
**Purpose:** Show the pathway from low blood pressure → renin release → aldosterone → salt/water recovery
**Key steps:**
1. Blood pressure drops (or Na+ delivery to DCT drops)
2. Juxtaglomerular cells release **renin**
3. Renin cleaves **angiotensinogen** (liver) → **Angiotensin I**
4. ACE (lungs) → **Angiotensin II**
5. Angiotensin II causes:
   a. Systemic vasoconstriction (immediate BP increase)
   b. Stimulation of adrenal cortex → **Aldosterone** release
6. Aldosterone increases Na+ channels and ATPases in DCT and collecting duct
7. More Na+ reabsorbed, water follows, blood volume increases

**Caption for chapter:** "The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) regulates blood pressure through a hormone cascade. When blood pressure or sodium levels drop, juxtaglomerular cells release renin, which initiates a cascade leading to the production of angiotensin II, a potent vasoconstrictor that immediately raises blood pressure. Angiotensin II also stimulates aldosterone release, which promotes sodium reabsorption in the collecting duct, increasing blood volume and providing a longer-term pressure support. This system activates within minutes and shuts down as blood pressure and sodium levels normalize."

### Figure 9: Bicarbonate Recovery in Proximal Tubule (Acid-Base Regulation)
**Type:** Diagram (cell cross-section with ion movements and enzyme reaction)
**Purpose:** Show how bicarbonate is recovered using carbonic anhydrase
**Key elements:**
- Lumen of PCT with HCO3− and H+ from filtrate
- Carbonic anhydrase enzyme (membrane-bound and intracellular)
- HCO3− + H+ reaction pathway: HCO3− + H+ ↔ H2CO3 ↔ CO2 + H2O
- CO2 diffusing into cell
- Reverse reaction inside cell producing HCO3−
- HCO3− cotransported with Na+ across basal membrane
- Na+/H+ antiporter recycling H+ back to lumen

**Caption for chapter:** "Bicarbonate recovery is essential for maintaining blood pH. In the proximal tubule lumen, hydrogen ions (H+) combine with filtered bicarbonate (HCO3−) to form carbonic acid. The enzyme carbonic anhydrase catalyzes its decomposition to CO2 and water. CO2 diffuses into the tubular cell, where the reverse reaction regenerates bicarbonate. This HCO3− is cotransported with sodium across the basal membrane back into the blood. This system recovers 80–90 percent of filtered bicarbonate, maintaining the blood's buffering capacity."

### Figure 10: Summary Table - Nephron Segments and Their Functions
**Type:** Table (matrix format)
**Purpose:** Quick reference showing what each segment reabsorbs, secretes, and what controls it
**Columns:** Segment | Main Reabsorbed | Main Secreted | Mechanism | Hormone Control | Result

| Segment | Reabsorbed | Secreted | Transport | Hormonal Control | Result |
|---------|-----------|----------|-----------|------------------|--------|
| **Glomerulus** | — | — | Filtration (pressure) | — | ~180 L filtrate/day |
| **PCT** | Glucose (100%), amino acids (100%), Na+ (67%), HCO3− (80%), water (67%) | Creatinine, drugs, H+ | Active & passive transport | — | Isotonic filtrate; recovery of ~2/3 of water & Na+ |
| **Loop of Henle** | Na+, Cl−, water | — | Active (ascending), osmosis (descending) | — | Hyperosmotic medulla (1200), hypo-osmotic filtrate (100) |
| **DCT** | Na+, Ca++, Mg++ | K+, H+ | Active transport | Aldosterone (Na+), PTH (Ca++) | Further fine-tuning of Na+ and Ca++ |
| **Collecting Duct** | Variable water, variable Na+ | K+, H+, NH4+ | Osmosis (water), active (ions) | ADH (water), Aldosterone (Na+) | Final urine composition; osmolarity 100–1200 depending on hydration |

**Caption:** "Each segment of the nephron has specialized functions. The glomerulus filters; the proximal tubule recovers bulk amounts of needed solutes and water; the loop of Henle creates an osmotic gradient; the distal tubule and collecting duct fine-tune water and electrolyte recovery under hormonal control."

## Image Sourcing Notes

All figures should be anatomically accurate and consistent with OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology (the source material). Standard conventions:
- Veins shown in blue, arteries in red
- Solute movement shown with arrows (active transport = thick, passive = thin)
- Osmolarity gradients shown with color gradient (lighter = lower osmolarity, darker = higher)
- Hormone names labeled at their site of action

High-quality versions should be at least 300 dpi for print; web versions at 150 dpi.
