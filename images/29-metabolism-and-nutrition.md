# Images: 29-Metabolism-and-Nutrition

Visual aids and figure specifications for the chapter.

## Figures in Chapter

### Core Pathway Figures

1. **[FIGURE: Glycolysis Overview]**
   - Shows the 10-step pathway from glucose to pyruvate
   - Highlights energy-consuming phase (steps 1-3, inputs ATP, generates G6P and F1,6BP)
   - Highlights energy-yielding phase (steps 4-10, outputs ATP and NADH, generates pyruvate)
   - Enzymes labeled: hexokinase, phosphofructokinase, aldolase, glyceraldehyde-3-P dehydrogenase, pyruvate kinase
   - Net equation shown: Glucose + 2ATP + 2NAD+ → 2 Pyruvate + 4ATP + 2NADH
   - Purpose: Shows the 10-step mechanism and the two phases conceptually
   - Modification for Attenborough × Feynman: Emphasize that electrons are extracted (reduction of NAD+) and that ATP is first spent then generated. Show arrows of energy flow, not just molecular transformations.

2. **[FIGURE: Krebs Cycle]**
   - Shows the 8-step cycle starting with acetyl CoA joining oxaloacetate to form citrate
   - Includes all intermediates: citrate → isocitrate → α-ketoglutarate → succinyl CoA → succinate → fumarate → malate → oxaloacetate
   - Highlights where CO₂ is released (from isocitrate to α-KG, and from α-KG to succinyl CoA)
   - Highlights where electrons are extracted (NADH and FADH₂ production)
   - ATP/GTP production noted (from succinyl CoA → succinate)
   - Enzymes labeled: citrate synthase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, succinyl CoA dehydrogenase, malate dehydrogenase
   - Net equation shown: Acetyl CoA + 3NAD+ + FAD + ADP → 3NADH + FADH₂ + ATP + 2CO₂
   - Purpose: Shows that the cycle returns to the starting point, extracts electrons (NADH, FADH₂), and removes carbons (CO₂)
   - Modification: Add annotation showing which step corresponds to pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDH, the conversion of pyruvate → acetyl CoA, which happens before the cycle starts)

3. **[FIGURE: Electron Transport Chain and Oxidative Phosphorylation]**
   - Shows the inner mitochondrial membrane with four protein complexes (I-IV) embedded
   - Electrons flow from NADH (Complex I) and FADH₂ (Complex II) through ubiquinone and cytochrome c to oxygen at Complex IV
   - At each complex, protons are pumped into the intermembrane space
   - Proton gradient is shown as a "battery" (higher [H+] in intermembrane space, lower in matrix)
   - ATP synthase is shown as a turbine spanning the membrane, with protons flowing through and ATP being synthesized
   - The final step: electrons + protons + oxygen → water
   - Yield noted: ~3 ATP per NADH, ~2 ATP per FADH₂
   - Purpose: Shows the mechanism of oxidative phosphorylation and why oxygen is essential
   - Modification: Emphasize that the gradient (not ATP itself) is the intermediate energy currency. Show the mechanical analogy of a turbine.

### Metabolic States Figures

4. **[FIGURE: Absorptive State (Fed)]**
   - Shows the fed state 2-3 hours after a meal
   - Highlights: rising blood glucose, insulin release, glucose uptake by liver/muscle/adipose, glycogenesis, protein synthesis, lipogenesis
   - Shows glucose flowing from intestines (via chylomicrons for fats, via portal blood for glucose) to liver, then to liver, muscle, and adipose tissue
   - Insulin signaling shown as activating GLUT4 movement and key enzymes (glycogen synthase, acetyl CoA carboxylase)
   - Time frame noted: ~3-4 hours after meal
   - Purpose: Shows the metabolic response to feeding
   - Modification: Show insulin as the master switch—all the metabolic changes are driven by insulin's presence

5. **[FIGURE: Postabsorptive State (Fasting)]**
   - Shows the fasting state 8-16 hours after eating
   - Highlights: falling blood glucose, glucagon release, glycogenolysis (first 4-8 hours), gluconeogenesis (continuing), lipolysis, fatty acid oxidation in muscle/heart, ketogenesis
   - Shows glucagon signaling (activating glycogen phosphorylase, inhibiting glycogen synthase, activating hormone-sensitive lipase)
   - Shows glucose production in liver from three sources: glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis from lactate/alanine/glycerol
   - Shows fatty acids released from adipose and taken up by muscle/heart for β-oxidation
   - Time frame noted: glycogen depleted by 4-8 hours, then gluconeogenesis sustains
   - Purpose: Shows the metabolic switch from storage to mobilization
   - Modification: Show the shift from glucose-dominant (early fasting) to fat-dominant (late fasting) fuel use

6. **[FIGURE: Starvation State]**
   - Shows the starvation state after 24-36 hours without food
   - Highlights: glycogen depleted, ketogenesis active, ketone bodies in blood, brain and heart using ketones, glucose reserved for brain and red blood cells
   - Shows the hierarchy: fat mobilized preferentially, muscle protein spared until fat is depleted
   - Shows ketone bodies produced in liver from excess acetyl CoA, circulating to brain/heart/muscle
   - Time frame noted: significant shift to ketones by day 3-4
   - Purpose: Shows the ultimate metabolic adaptation to sustained energy deficit
   - Modification: Emphasize that this is not a failure state but a sophisticated survival mechanism

7. **[FIGURE: Metabolic Cycling—The Three States in Sequence]**
   - Shows the daily metabolic cycle: fed (breakfast/lunch/dinner) → postabsorptive (between meals, overnight) → fed again
   - Shows how blood glucose, insulin, and glucagon oscillate
   - Shows how substrate use shifts (glucose-dominant → fat-dominant → glucose-dominant)
   - Purpose: Integrates the three states into a realistic daily rhythm
   - Modification for this chapter: Not explicitly shown in the original source but implied in section 6. Could be added as a summary figure.

### Thermoregulation Figures

8. **[FIGURE: Hypothalamus as Thermostat]**
   - Shows the hypothalamus in the brain with thermoreceptor inputs (skin and core temperature sensors)
   - Shows the set-point (37°C) and negative feedback loop
   - If temperature falls: outputs signal → shivering, non-shivering thermogenesis, vasoconstriction, thyroid hormone increase
   - If temperature rises: outputs signal → sweating, vasodilation, behavioral cooling
   - Purpose: Shows the thermoregulatory control system
   - Modification: Emphasize the negative feedback nature (deviation → correction → return to set-point)

9. **[FIGURE: Heat Exchange Mechanisms]**
   - Shows the four heat exchange pathways: radiation (~60%), evaporation (~22%), convection (~15%), conduction (~3%)
   - Shows a human body with radiative heat loss as infrared waves, evaporative loss as sweat/water vapor, convective loss as warm air rising, conductive loss as contact with cold object
   - Environmental conditions shown: temperature gradient, humidity, wind, water
   - Purpose: Shows the mechanisms by which the body loses (or gains) heat
   - Modification: Show how each mechanism is compromised in extreme environments (radiation ineffective in room-temperature air; evaporation ineffective in humid heat; conduction dangerous in cold water)

10. **[FIGURE: ATP Synthesis and Heat Production]**
    - Shows mitochondrial ATP synthesis coupled with heat release
    - ~40% of energy captured as ATP, ~60% released as heat
    - Shows that heat is not wasted; it's what maintains core temperature
    - Links ATP synthesis to thermoregulation: the same mitochondrial process produces both ATP and body heat
    - Purpose: Shows that thermoregulation is not separate from metabolism but intimately linked
    - Modification: Could show side-by-side comparison of normal ATP synthase (proton gradient drives ATP synthesis) vs. uncoupling protein in brown fat (proton gradient dissipated as heat without ATP synthesis)

## Alternative Visual Approaches

1. **Animated sequence:** Rather than static figures, an animated sequence showing electrons moving through the ETC, or a time-lapse of metabolic switching through the three states, could enhance understanding. Not feasible in a static chapter but noted for future multimedia versions.

2. **Comparative metabolic maps:** Side-by-side comparison of fed vs. fasted states, showing which pathways are active/inactive. This is present in the original source; maintained in this conversion.

3. **Energy flow diagram:** Rather than molecular structures, a simplified diagram showing energy flow (food → ATP, ATP → work/heat, heat → environment) might complement the mechanistic figures. Not included here because the chapter already emphasizes first principles.

## Figures NOT Included (and Why)

- **ATP structure:** The original source shows the adenine ring, ribose, and three phosphate groups. The chapter mentions that the high-energy bond is between the second and third phosphates, but doesn't require readers to memorize the structure. Omitted to avoid non-essential detail.
- **Vitamin and mineral tables:** The original source includes detailed tables of vitamins A-K and trace minerals. The chapter de-emphasizes vitamin/mineral metabolism and defers to reference materials. Tables excluded.
- **MyPlate diagram:** The original includes the USDA MyPlate visual. The chapter de-emphasizes dietary guidelines in favor of mechanistic understanding. Excluded.
- **Cushing syndrome and Addison's disease:** These pathology boxes appear in the original source. Mechanistic concepts are integrated into the chapter text (cortisol's role in gluconeogenesis, the urea cycle's role in nitrogen disposal) without dedicated figures.
