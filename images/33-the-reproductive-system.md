# Figures & Visual Assets: Chapter 33 (Reproductive System)

## Overview

This chapter requires 8-10 figures to support three concepts and the integration section. Figures serve as visual anchors for readers to follow complex anatomy, cycle timing, and hormonal feedback loops.

## Required Figures by Section

### Concept 1: Gametogenesis

**[FIGURE 1.1] Meiosis I and Meiosis II: The Two Divisions**

*Purpose*: Show side-by-side how meiosis I separates chromosome pairs and meiosis II separates replicated chromosomes.

*Format*: Labeled diagram with two columns.
- Left column: Diploid cell (46 chromosomes shown as 23 pairs) → DNA replication → Meiosis I (pairs separate) → Two haploid cells (23 chromosomes each, replicated).
- Right column: Each haploid cell → Meiosis II (replicated chromosomes separate) → Four haploid cells (23 chromosomes each, unreplicated).

*Key labels*: Homologous pairs, replicated chromosomes (sister chromatids), centromere, spindle fiber, cytokinesis.

*Audience note*: Readers have seen mitosis (Chapter 6) and understand chromosome replication. This builds on that.

---

**[FIGURE 1.2] Spermatogenesis: From Stem Cell to Motile Sperm**

*Purpose*: Follow one sperm's development from spermatogonium to spermatozoon, showing the timeline and key cell types.

*Format*: Vertical timeline (0 to 64 days) with branching pathways showing mitosis, meiosis I, meiosis II, and spermiogenesis.

*Key stages*:
- Spermatogonium (stem cell, basement membrane).
- Primary spermatocyte (diploid, pre-meiosis I).
- Secondary spermatocyte (haploid, post-meiosis I, two per primary).
- Spermatid (haploid, post-meiosis II, four per primary).
- Spermatozoon (mature sperm, after spermiogenesis).

*Timeline annotations*:
- Mitosis: 1 day.
- Meiosis I to II: ~2 weeks.
- Spermiogenesis: ~2 weeks.
- Total: ~64 days.
- New cycle start: every ~16 days.

*Key labels*: Nucleus, mitochondria, flagellum, acrosome. Show the dramatic transformation from round cell to streamlined sperm.

---

**[FIGURE 1.3] Oogenesis: Arrest, Release, and Completion**

*Purpose*: Show the long arrest periods and asymmetric division in oogenesis, contrasting with spermatogenesis.

*Format*: Timeline spanning fetal life to adulthood, with key events marked and cell divisions highlighted.

*Key stages*:
- Oogonium (stem cell, mitotic division during fetal life).
- Primary oocyte (arrested in prophase I of meiosis I, from fetal life until ovulation).
- Secondary oocyte (after meiosis I, arrested in metaphase II, released at ovulation).
- Ovum (after meiosis II, only completed if fertilized).

*Arrest periods*:
- Primary oocyte arrest: years to decades.
- Secondary oocyte arrest: hours to days (until fertilization).

*Asymmetric division*:
- Meiosis I: 90% cytoplasm to secondary oocyte, 10% to first polar body.
- Meiosis II: 90% cytoplasm to ovum, 10% to second polar body.
- Show polar bodies degenerating.

*Timeline annotations*:
- Oogonia divide during fetal life (weeks 8-20 of gestation).
- Primary oocytes formed by ~5-7 months gestation, then arrest.
- Oocytes remain arrested from fetal life until ovulation (could be 12-50+ years later).
- One ovulation per cycle (~28 days) from puberty to menopause.

*Key labels*: Nucleus, cytoplasm (show relative size), zona pellucida (when present), corona radiata (when present), polar bodies.

**[FIGURE 2.4] Stages of Folliculogenesis: Primordial to Tertiary Follicle**

*Purpose*: Show the growth and maturation of a follicle from resting to ovulation-ready.

*Format*: Linear progression showing 5-6 stages side by side.

*Stages*:
1. Primordial follicle: Oocyte + 1 layer flat (squamous) granulosa cells. Resting state.
2. Primary follicle: Oocyte + 1 layer cuboidal granulosa cells (actively dividing). Granulosa cells enlarging.
3. Early secondary follicle: Oocyte + multiple layers of cuboidal granulosa cells. Theca cells beginning to wrap around.
4. Late secondary follicle: Oocyte + multiple granulosa layers + theca layer. Antrum (fluid) beginning to form.
5. Tertiary follicle: Oocyte surrounded by multiple granulosa layers. Large antrum (fluid-filled chamber). Theca cells fully developed.

*Key labels*:
- Oocyte nucleus (large, central).
- Zona pellucida (acellular glycoprotein layer around oocyte).
- Granulosa cells (secreting estrogen).
- Theca cells (outer layer, producing hormones).
- Antrum (follicular fluid).

*Annotations*:
- Follicle diameter at each stage (primordial ~30 micrometers, tertiary ~10+ millimeters).
- Proliferation rates (granulosa cell divisions increase, theca cells increase).
- Hormone production (estrogen produced, increases over stages).

**[FIGURE 2.5] Spermatozoon Structure: Anatomy of a Mature Sperm**

*Purpose*: Show the detailed anatomy of a single sperm and relate structure to function.

*Format*: Longitudinal (side) cross-section of a single sperm with labels and callout boxes.

*Key regions*:
- **Head** (~5 micrometers long): Nucleus (compact, condensed chromatin). Acrosome (enzyme-filled cap covering anterior nucleus).
- **Midpiece** (~5 micrometers): Mitochondria tightly wrapped in spiral. Centriole (base of flagellum).
- **Tail** (~50 micrometers): Axial filament (9+2 microtubule arrangement for movement).

*Function callouts*:
- Nucleus: carries DNA.
- Acrosome: enzymes to penetrate zona pellucida.
- Mitochondria: ATP production for flagellar beating.
- Flagellum: propels sperm.

*Size reference*: Show sperm compared to a red blood cell or nucleus for scale.

### Concept 3: Hormonal Control

**[FIGURE 3.1] The HPG Axis: Feedback Loops**

*Purpose*: Show the three-level system (hypothalamus, pituitary, gonads) and feedback pathways.

*Format*: Diagram with three boxes (hypothalamus, anterior pituitary, gonad) and arrows showing hormone release and feedback.

*Hypothalamus box*:
- Releases GnRH (pulsatile, every 60-90 minutes).
- Inhibited by negative feedback from high sex hormones.

*Anterior pituitary box*:
- Releases FSH and LH in response to GnRH.
- Inhibited by negative feedback from high sex hormones.

*Gonad box* (split into male and female):
- **Male**: LH → Leydig cells → testosterone. FSH → Sertoli cells → spermatogenesis (and inhibin production).
- **Female**: LH → theca cells → androgens (converted to estrogen). FSH → granulosa cells → estrogen and inhibin.

*Feedback arrows*:
- Negative feedback: testosterone/estrogen/progesterone inhibit GnRH and LH (solid lines, "—|" notation).
- Positive feedback (ovulation only): high estrogen amplifies LH and FSH (dashed line in females, "→→" notation, around day 13-14).

*Color code*: GnRH in blue, FSH in green, LH in red, testosterone in orange (male), estrogen in pink (female), progesterone in purple (female).

**[FIGURE 3.2] Hormone Levels During the Menstrual Cycle**

*Purpose*: Show the rising and falling of FSH, LH, estrogen, and progesterone over a 28-day cycle.

*Format*: Line graph with days (1-28) on x-axis and hormone concentration (arbitrary units) on y-axis.

*Four curves*:
- **FSH** (green line): Low during menses, rises slowly during follicular phase, peaks at ovulation (small peak), falls during luteal phase.
- **LH** (red line): Low during follicular phase, dramatic spike around day 14 (LH surge, the highest point on graph), falls during luteal phase.
- **Estrogen** (pink line): Low at menses, rises during follicular phase, peaks just before LH surge (day 13), dips at ovulation, rises again early luteal phase (corpus luteum produces some), then gradually declines.
- **Progesterone** (purple line): ~0 during menses and early follicular phase, rises sharply after ovulation (day 14+), peaks mid-luteal phase, crashes just before menses.

*Cycle phases marked on x-axis*:
- Menses (days 1-5): shaded light gray.
- Follicular phase (days 6-13): shaded light blue.
- Ovulation (day 14): marked with vertical line and "ovulation" label.
- Luteal phase (days 15-28): shaded light orange.

*Key events annotated*:
- "LH surge triggers ovulation" (at day 14, LH peak).
- "Corpus luteum forms" (after day 14, progesterone rises).
- "Corpus luteum degrades" (around day 26, progesterone crashes).
- "Menstruation begins" (day 28/1, endometrium sheds).

**[FIGURE 3.3] Ovarian and Menstrual Cycles: Synchronized Events**

*Purpose*: Show how ovarian cycle (follicle and corpus luteum changes) synchronizes with menstrual cycle (endometrial changes) over 28 days.

*Format*: Two-track timeline showing both cycles simultaneously.

*Top track (Ovarian cycle)*:
- Primordial and growing follicles (days 1-13).
- Dominant follicle (days 8-14).
- Ovulation (day 14).
- Corpus luteum (days 15-28).
- Corpus albicans (days 26+).

*Bottom track (Menstrual cycle)*:
- Menses: endometrium shedding (days 1-5). Shown as tissue breaking down.
- Proliferative phase: endometrium thickening (days 6-13). Shown as lining building up.
- Secretory phase: endometrium preparing for implantation (days 14-28). Shown as lining fully thickened with glands and spiral arteries.

*Connecting arrows*:
- Estrogen from growing follicles (days 1-13) → thickens endometrium (proliferative phase).
- Progesterone from corpus luteum (days 15-28) → primes endometrium (secretory phase).
- Progesterone crash (day 26+) → endometrial shedding (menses begins).

*Color scheme*:
- Follicles in light orange, corpus luteum in dark orange.
- Endometrium in red/pink, thinning to light at menses, building through proliferative, fully thickened during secretory.

**[FIGURE 3.4] The LH Surge and Ovulation: Mechanism**

*Purpose*: Show the hormonal switch from negative to positive feedback that triggers the LH surge and ovulation.

*Format*: Two side-by-side panels showing "Day 13 (pre-ovulation)" and "Day 14 (ovulation)."

*Panel 1 (Day 13)*:
- Dominant follicle producing high estrogen.
- Estrogen shown at threshold level (indicated by a "threshold line" graphic).
- Estrogen level below threshold → negative feedback on hypothalamus and pituitary → inhibit GnRH, FSH, LH.
- Result: Most follicles undergo atresia; only dominant follicle survives.

*Panel 2 (Day 14)*:
- Dominant follicle producing very high estrogen (exceeds threshold).
- Estrogen level above threshold → POSITIVE feedback on hypothalamus and pituitary → amplify GnRH, FSH, LH.
- LH surge shown as dramatic spike (taller than any other time in cycle).
- LH surge triggers: 1) Meiosis I completion in oocyte. 2) Proteases break down ovary wall. 3) Antrum pressure ruptures ovary. 4) Secondary oocyte expelled.

*Annotations*:
- "Feedback switch" label at the threshold line.
- Arrows showing positive feedback amplification (thicker, double-headed arrows).
- Ovary anatomy detail showing follicle rupturing and oocyte being released.

## Integration Section Figure

**[FIGURE 4] The Journey from Sperm to Implantation**

*Purpose*: Timeline showing the 3-5 day journey of a sperm and oocyte from ejaculation and ovulation to implantation.

*Format*: Horizontal timeline with 7 key events marked.

*Events*:
1. **Ejaculation (Hour 0)**: Sperm deposited in vagina. Semen coagulates briefly, then liquefies. Sperm begin navigating toward cervix.
2. **Cervical crossing (Hours 1-6)**: Sperm pass through cervical mucus (thin and stretchy at ovulation, facilitates passage).
3. **Uterine transport (Hours 6-24)**: Myometrial contractions propel sperm toward uterine tube. Prostaglandins in semen trigger some contractions.
4. **Uterine tube residence (Hours 6-24)**: Sperm reside in uterine tube, undergo capacitation (biochemical priming). Oocyte is also in ampulla of tube.
5. **Fertilization (Hours 24-36)**: Sperm and oocyte meet in ampulla. One sperm penetrates zona pellucida. Sperm nucleus and oocyte nucleus fuse. Zygote forms.
6. **Zygote division and transport (Days 1-3)**: Zygote divides (2-cell, 4-cell, 8-cell, morula, blastocyst). Muscular contractions and cilia move blastocyst toward uterus.
7. **Implantation (Days 3-5)**: Blastocyst reaches uterus. Endometrium is primed (secretory phase, thickened, well-vascularized). Blastocyst implants into endometrium. Trophoblast cells begin producing hCG.

*Key structures shown at each event*:
- Sperm anatomy (flagellum beating).
- Egg anatomy (zona pellucida, corona radiata).
- Uterine tube (showing muscular wall, cilia).
- Uterine interior (showing endometrium thickness, blood vessels).

*Size indicators*: Show relative sizes of sperm, oocyte, zygote, and blastocyst at each stage.

## Figure Captions (Concise)

1. **Meiosis I and Meiosis II**: Two rounds of cell division with different purposes. Meiosis I separates chromosome pairs; meiosis II separates replicated chromosomes.

2. **Spermatogenesis**: Sperm develop over 64 days from spermatogonium to spermatozoon. New cycles begin every 16 days.

3. **Oogenesis**: Primary oocytes arrest in meiosis I during fetal life and remain arrested for years. Meiosis I resumes just before ovulation, meiosis II only after fertilization.

4. **Male reproductive system**: Path of sperm from testis through accessory glands and penis. Three glands contribute secretions: seminal vesicles (60%), prostate (30%), bulbourethral (small amount).

5. **Seminiferous tubule cross-section**: Spermatogonia at basement membrane mature toward lumen. Sertoli cells support germ cells. Blood-testis barrier protects developing sperm.

6. **Female reproductive system**: Ovary releases oocyte at ovulation. Fimbriae catch oocyte and guide it into uterine tube. Uterine tube transports developing embryo to uterus.

7. **Folliculogenesis**: Primordial follicles grow through primary, secondary, and tertiary stages. Most undergo atresia; one becomes dominant and ovulates.

8. **Spermatozoon anatomy**: Compact head (nucleus + acrosome), midpiece (mitochondria), and tail (flagellum) enable sperm to navigate female tract and penetrate oocyte.

9. **HPG axis**: Three-level feedback system with negative feedback as default and positive feedback at ovulation.

10. **Hormone levels**: FSH, LH, estrogen, and progesterone rise and fall in coordinated pattern over 28-day cycle.

11. **Ovarian and menstrual cycles**: Synchronized events: follicle growth drives endometrial proliferation; corpus luteum drives endometrial secretion; corpus luteum degradation triggers shedding.

12. **LH surge mechanism**: Feedback switches from negative to positive when estrogen exceeds threshold, triggering LH surge and ovulation.

13. **Journey to implantation**: Sperm and oocyte meet in uterine tube, fertilize, divide, travel to uterus, and implant in primed endometrium.

## Notes on Visual Clarity

- **Color consistency**: Use the same color for each hormone/structure across all figures (e.g., estrogen always pink, LH always red).
- **Labels**: All anatomical structures should be labeled clearly. Avoid overcrowding.
- **Arrows**: Use different arrow styles to distinguish different processes (solid for direct effects, dashed for feedback, double for amplification).
- **Scale**: Include size references (micrometer scale) for cells and organelles.
- **Legends**: Provide legends for all color-coded diagrams.
