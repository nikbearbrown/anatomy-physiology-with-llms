### Concept 2: Action Potential

**[FIGURE: Resting Membrane Potential]**
- Cell membrane with ion pumps (Na+/K+ ATPase)
- Ion concentrations: high K+ inside, high Na+ outside
- Voltage across membrane: -70 mV inside relative to outside
- Show selectivity of membrane: permeable to K+ at rest, not Na+

**[FIGURE: Action Potential - Voltage Over Time]**
- Y-axis: membrane potential (-90 to +30 mV)
- X-axis: time (milliseconds)
- Curve showing: resting potential → threshold (-55 mV) → depolarization to +30 mV → repolarization → hyperpolarization → return to resting
- Overlay: Na+ channel open/closed, K+ channel open/closed states
- Label: threshold, peak, repolarization, hyperpolarization, refractory period (absolute and relative)

**[FIGURE: Voltage-Gated Channels - Three States]**
- Sodium channel: closed (resting) → open (active) → inactivated (refractory)
- Potassium channel: closed (resting) → open (delayed) → closed (recovery)
- Show timing: Na+ opens fast, K+ opens slow, Na+ inactivates before K+ fully closes
- This creates the characteristic action potential shape

**[FIGURE: Propagation Along Unmyelinated Axon]**
- Series of membrane cross-sections showing depolarization spreading
- Panel 1: threshold reached at one location
- Panel 2: Na+ channels open at that location, voltage becomes positive
- Panel 3: voltage change spreads to neighboring membrane (passive spread)
- Panel 4: neighboring channels open
- Show decrement: voltage change is smaller at each successive location (resistive loss)

**[FIGURE: Saltatory Conduction - Myelinated Axon]**
- Series showing node-to-node propagation
- Myelin segments (thick shading) with gaps at nodes (unshaded)
- Depolarization confined to nodes (high channel density there)
- Voltage change is large enough to reach neighboring node
- Regeneration at each node → fast conduction (100 m/s vs. 0.5 m/s unmyelinated)
- Show timing: action potential at node 1 → action potential at node 2 (nanoseconds apart)

**[FIGURE: Refractory Periods]**
- Stimulus 1: triggers action potential normally
- Stimulus 2 (during absolute refractory period): no response, even with strong stimulus
- Stimulus 3 (during relative refractory period): requires stronger stimulus to trigger action potential
- Y-axis: membrane potential; X-axis: time
- Explanation: Na+ channel inactivation during absolute; hyperpolarization during relative

### Concept 3: Synaptic Transmission

**[FIGURE: The Synapse - Anatomy]**
- Presynaptic terminal (end bulb of axon)
  - Synaptic vesicles (neurotransmitter storage)
  - Mitochondria (energy)
  - Calcium channels (voltage-gated)
  - SNARE proteins (on membrane)
- Synaptic cleft (~20 nm)
- Postsynaptic membrane
  - Receptors (neurotransmitter recognition)
  - Ion channels (opened by receptor)
  - Reuptake transporters (cleanup)

**[FIGURE: Synaptic Transmission - Steps]**
- Frame 1: Action potential arrives at presynaptic terminal
- Frame 2: Voltage-gated Ca2+ channels open, Ca2+ enters
- Frame 3: SNARE proteins bring vesicle to membrane, fusion begins
- Frame 4: Vesicle empties, neurotransmitter molecules released into cleft
- Frame 5: Neurotransmitter diffuses across cleft and binds to receptors
- Frame 6: Postsynaptic ion channels open, current flows
- Timeline: 1 ms arrival, ~100 microseconds exocytosis, ~1 ms diffusion and binding

**[FIGURE: Neurotransmitter Receptors and Effects]**
- Excitatory synapse: glutamate binds AMPA/NMDA receptors → Na+ channels open → inward current → depolarization (EPSC/EPSP)
- Inhibitory synapse: GABA/glycine binds to Cl-/K+ channels → outward or inward current (hyperpolarization or shunting) → hyperpolarization (IPSC/IPSP)
- Size comparison: single EPSP ~0.5 mV vs. threshold ~15 mV → many synapses must fire together

**[FIGURE: EPSC/IPSC Time Course]**
- Excitatory: rapid rise (1-2 ms), decay (10-20 ms) — slower than receptor binding alone because membrane acts as capacitor
- Inhibitory: similar time course but inverted (hyperpolarization instead of depolarization)
- Show why temporal summation works: if inputs arrive within the decay time, their effects add

**[FIGURE: Spatial Integration - Soma Receiving Multiple Inputs]**
- Dendrite 1 (distal): weak EPSC due to voltage attenuation (exponential decay with distance)
- Dendrite 2 (proximal): strong EPSC due to proximity to soma
- Soma integrates both (spatial summation)
- Axon hillock: if sum exceeds threshold, action potential fires
- Show why dendritic tree structure matters: branching increases receptive area, but distance from soma reduces effect

**[FIGURE: Temporal Integration - Summation Example]**
- Inputs arriving close in time: voltages overlap, sum to suprathreshold
- Inputs arriving far apart: individual EPSCs too small and decay completely before next arrives
- This shows why timing of inputs matters, not just number of inputs

**[FIGURE: Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)]**
- Panel A: Single presynaptic stimulation alone (weak effect on postsynaptic voltage)
- Panel B: Presynaptic stimulation while postsynaptic neuron is depolarized by other inputs
  - NMDA receptors open (only when postsynaptic is depolarized)
  - Ca2+ enters → biochemical cascades
  - More AMPA receptors inserted into postsynaptic membrane
- Panel C: Later, same presynaptic stimulation has larger effect (synapse strengthened)
- Show the "Hebbian" logic: presynaptic neuron fires while postsynaptic fires → credit assignment → strengthen connection

### Integration and Pathways

**[FIGURE: Summary - From Neuron to Network]**
- Single neuron: soma + dendrites + axon
- Network: multiple neurons connected at synapses
- Show directional flow (dendrite → soma → axon → synapse → next dendrite)
- Show integration at soma (multiple synapses summate)
- Show plasticity at synapse (LTP/LTD changes connection strength)

**[FIGURE: Conduction Velocity Comparison]**
- Chart showing:
  - Unmyelinated small (sensory): 0.5 m/s
  - Unmyelinated large (autonomic): 1-2 m/s
  - Myelinated small: 10-50 m/s
  - Myelinated large (motor): 50-120 m/s
- Show relationship to axon diameter and myelin

**[FIGURE: Ion Channel States Through Action Potential]**
- Time course aligned with voltage trace
- Resting: Na+ closed, K+ closed
- Depolarization to threshold: Na+ begins to open
- Peak depolarization: Na+ open (inactivating), K+ opening
- Repolarization: Na+ inactivated, K+ fully open
- Return to rest: K+ closing, Na+ recovering from inactivation
- Refractory: Na+ still inactivated (absolute), then recovering with K+ still open (relative)
