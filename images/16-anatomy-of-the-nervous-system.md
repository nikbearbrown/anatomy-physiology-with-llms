# Images and Figures: Chapter 16 — Anatomy of the Nervous System

## Figures referenced or needed for chapter 16

### Figure 1: Neural Tube Folding (developmental sequence)
**Source:** Implied by 02-m46535.md description of neural groove, neural folds, neural tube formation.
**What it shows:** Three or four time-lapse panels showing ectoderm → neural plate → neural groove with folds → neural tube closure.
**Caption suggestion:** "The neural groove folds inward and closes to form the neural tube, the precursor of the entire nervous system."
**Integration point:** Concept 1, mechanism section.

### Figure 2: Primary and Secondary Vesicles
**Source:** Referenced as "fig-ch13_01_02" in source 02-m46535.md.
**What it shows:** Side-by-side schematic of (left) three primary vesicles labeled prosencephalon, mesencephalon, rhombencephalon at week 4; (right) five secondary vesicles labeled telencephalon, diencephalon, mesencephalon, metencephalon, myelencephalon at week 6.
**Caption suggestion:** "Three primary vesicles differentiate into five secondary vesicles, which map directly to adult brain regions."
**Integration point:** Concept 1, mechanism section. Critical for the "three to five" mapping.

### Figure 3: Adult Brain Regions (lateral view)
**Source:** Implied by 03-m46533.md descriptions of lobes, sulci, gyri.
**What it shows:** Lateral view of brain with four lobes labeled (frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital), major sulci marked (central, lateral, parieto-occipital).
**Caption suggestion:** "The cerebral cortex is divided into four lobes by major folds (sulci). The central sulcus separates frontal and parietal lobes."
**Integration point:** Concept 2, mechanism section.

### Figure 4: Sagittal Section of Brain (midline view)
**Source:** Implied by descriptions of midbrain, pons, medulla, diencephalon, cerebellum positioning.
**What it shows:** Midline sagittal section with major regions labeled: cerebrum, diencephalon (with thalamus and hypothalamus identified), midbrain, pons, cerebellum, medulla, spinal cord.
**Caption suggestion:** "A sagittal section reveals how the brain regions stack from anterior (cerebrum) to posterior (medulla), and how the cerebellum attaches to the pons."
**Integration point:** Concept 2, mechanism section.

### Figure 5: Gray and White Matter Organization of Spinal Cord (cross-section)
**Source:** Referenced as "fig-ch13_02_09" in source 03-m46533.md.
**What it shows:** Cross-section of spinal cord with gray matter "H" labeled with posterior horn, anterior horn, lateral horn; white matter columns labeled posterior, lateral, anterior.
**Caption suggestion:** "The gray matter of the spinal cord is organized as an 'H' shape, with sensory input via the posterior horn and motor output via the anterior horn."
**Integration point:** Concept 2, spinal cord section.

### Figure 6: Spinal Nerve and Dorsal Root Ganglion
**Source:** Referenced as "fig-ch13_04_01" and "fig-ch13_04_02" in source 05-m46553.md.
**What it shows:** Schematic showing a spinal nerve attached to the spinal cord, with dorsal and ventral roots visible, and the dorsal root ganglion as an enlargement on the dorsal root.
**Caption suggestion:** "A spinal nerve has two roots: the dorsal root carries sensory axons, and the ventral root carries motor axons. The dorsal root ganglion contains sensory neuron cell bodies."
**Integration point:** Concept 3, peripheral nervous system section.

### Figure 7: Nerve Plexuses
**Source:** Referenced as "fig-ch13_04_06" in source 05-m46553.md.
**What it shows:** Anatomical diagram showing the four nerve plexuses (cervical, brachial, lumbar, sacral) with their constituent spinal nerves and major systemic nerves (radial, median, ulnar, femoral, sciatic) labeled.
**Caption suggestion:** "Spinal nerves reorganize through plexuses to form systemic nerves that supply the limbs. Major nerves include the radial and ulnar (from brachial plexus) and the sciatic (from sacral plexus)."
**Integration point:** Concept 3, plexuses section.

### Figure 8: Dermatome Map
**Source:** Implied by 05-m46553.md description of dermatomes.
**What it shows:** Anterior and posterior views of the human body with dermatomes color-coded or labeled by spinal nerve level (C3, C4, T1-T12, L1-L5, S1-S5).
**Caption suggestion:** "Dermatomes are bands of skin supplied by sensory axons from a single spinal nerve. The pattern allows clinicians to localize nerve injuries based on sensory loss."
**Integration point:** Concept 3, worked example on dermatomes.

### Figure 9: The Twelve Cranial Nerves (inferior view of brain)
**Source:** Referenced as "fig-ch13_04_05" in source 05-m46553.md.
**What it shows:** Inferior (basal) view of the brain with the 12 cranial nerves emerging from the brain stem, numbered I-XII, with names and major branches visible.
**Caption suggestion:** "The 12 cranial nerves emerge from the brain. They control sensation and motor function of the head, neck, and (in the case of the vagus nerve) thoracic and abdominal organs."
**Integration point:** Concept 3, cranial nerves section.

### Figure 10: Visual System Pathway
**Source:** Not explicitly in source material, but implied by discussion of optic nerve (CN II), thalamus, superior colliculus, occipital cortex.
**What it shows:** Schematic showing retina → optic nerve (CN II) → lateral geniculate nucleus of thalamus → primary visual cortex in occipital lobe, with a side branch from optic nerve to superior colliculus (midbrain).
**Caption suggestion:** "The visual pathway exemplifies how embryological organization predicts adult connections: the retina, which develops as part of the diencephalon, projects to the diencephalon (thalamus) and the adjacent midbrain (superior colliculus)."
**Integration point:** Integration section, used as worked example of three organizational levels.

## Notes on figure sourcing

The source material references OpenStax CNX figures (e.g., fig-ch13_01_02). These are openly licensed and can be adapted. The chapter uses these where they are explicitly referenced in the source. Some figures (neural tube folding sequence, visual pathway schematic) are implied by the text but not explicitly shown in source and may need to be created or sourced from supplementary materials.

## Accessibility notes

All figures should include alt text and figure captions that do not rely on color alone to distinguish structures. The dermatome map, in particular, should have texture or pattern as well as color coding.
