### Concept 1: Hormones as Chemical Messages

**[FIGURE 1: Water-soluble vs. lipid-soluble hormone pathways]**
Two-panel diagram showing side-by-side comparison:
- **Left panel (water-soluble, e.g., epinephrine):** Hormone in bloodstream approaches cell. Receptor sits in cell membrane with extracellular binding domain. Hormone binds to receptor. Intracellular G protein activates. Adenylyl cyclase produces cAMP. Protein kinase phosphorylates downstream targets. Response occurs within seconds. Phosphodiesterase quickly degrades cAMP. Response ceases.
- **Right panel (lipid-soluble, e.g., cortisol):** Hormone in bloodstream crosses lipid bilayer. Receptor floats in cytoplasm or nucleus. Hormone-receptor complex forms. Complex travels to nucleus. Binds DNA promoter region. Gene transcription increases. mRNA synthesized. Protein production increases over minutes to hours. Response is sustained until hormone is degraded or cleared.
- Labels: hormone (red circle), receptor (blue shape), membrane (yellow), cAMP (small purple stars), DNA (blue helix), protein (green oval).

**[FIGURE 2: Epinephrine vs. cortisol in acute vs. chronic stress]**
Timeline diagram (0 min to 24 hr) showing two curves:
- **Epinephrine (red curve):** Sharp spike at 0 min, high for 1–2 min, drops back to baseline by 5 min. Label: "Fight-or-flight reflex: fast, brief."
- **Cortisol (blue curve):** Slower rise starting at 1 min, peaks at 20–30 min, remains elevated for 8–12 hours. Label: "Stress adaptation: slow, sustained."
- Annotations: "Threat perceived" at t=0, "Heart rate, breathing ↑" during epinephrine peak, "Glucose mobilization, immune shift" during cortisol elevation.

**[FIGURE 3: Water-soluble hormone cascade (generic example)]**
Flowchart showing: Stimulus → Hormone released → Hormone in blood → Receptor on cell surface → G protein activation → Second messenger (cAMP) ↑ → Protein kinase cascade → Cellular response (e.g., "glucose uptake," "enzyme activation," "ion channel opening"). Include time scale: "seconds to minutes."

**[FIGURE 4: Lipid-soluble hormone cascade (generic example)]**
Flowchart showing: Stimulus → Hormone synthesized and released → Hormone in blood → Hormone crosses cell membrane → Intracellular receptor binding → Hormone-receptor complex enters nucleus → Binds DNA → Gene transcription ↑ → mRNA, protein synthesis ↑ → Cellular response (e.g., "metabolic rate ↑," "growth," "anti-inflammatory"). Include time scale: "minutes to hours."

### Concept 2: The Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis

**[FIGURE 5: The hypothalamic-pituitary complex (anatomy)]**
Sagittal view of brain/skull showing:
- Hypothalamus (labeled, midline, sitting above pituitary)
- Thalamus (above hypothalamus)
- Optic chiasm (anterior to pituitary)
- Infundibulum (stalk connecting hypothalamus to pituitary, shown as thin tube)
- Anterior pituitary (glandular tissue, lower part of pituitary)
- Posterior pituitary (neural tissue, upper part of pituitary)
- Sella turcica (bony cup holding pituitary)
- Label: "Hypophyseal portal system: capillaries in infundibulum carry releasing hormones from hypothalamus to anterior pituitary."

**[FIGURE 6: Negative feedback loop in the thyroid axis]**
Circular diagram showing:
1. "Low blood T3/T4" (bottom of circle, red box)
2. Arrow up: "Hypothalamus releases TRH" (left side)
3. Arrow up: "Anterior pituitary releases TSH" (top left)
4. Arrow right: "Thyroid gland releases T3/T4" (top)
5. Arrow down/right: "Blood T3/T4 rises" (right side, green box, labeled "Feedback inhibition")
6. Inhibitory arrows from "Blood T3/T4" pointing back to "Hypothalamus" and "Anterior pituitary" (shown with ⊣ symbols, red X marks)
7. Complete loop: as T3/T4 rise, feedback suppresses further TRH and TSH. As T3/T4 fall, feedback weakens, allowing TRH and TSH to rise again.
- Time scale annotation: "Lag of 2–4 hours between TSH release and feedback."

**[FIGURE 7: The three major endocrine axes (comparative)]**
Three parallel columns, each showing a cascade:

| Thyroid Axis | Adrenal Axis | Gonadal Axis |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus: cold, need for ↑ metabolism | Stimulus: stress (physical, psychological) | Stimulus: puberty cue; pulsatile GnRH |
| Hypothalamus: TRH | Hypothalamus: CRH | Hypothalamus: GnRH (pulsatile ~1/90 min) |
| Ant. Pituitary: TSH | Ant. Pituitary: ACTH | Ant. Pituitary: FSH, LH |
| Target gland: Thyroid (T3/T4) | Target gland: Adrenal cortex (cortisol) | Target gland: Gonads (testosterone, estrogen, progesterone) |
| Effect: ↑ metabolic rate, heat | Effect: ↑ glucose, immune shift, stress response | Effect: sexual maturation, gamete production |
| Feedback: T3/T4 inhibit TRH, TSH | Feedback: cortisol inhibits CRH, ACTH | Feedback: sex hormones inhibit GnRH, FSH, LH |
| Duration: hours to days | Duration: hours to days | Duration: weeks to months (puberty); cyclical (female cycle) |

### Concept 3: Glucose Homeostasis

**[FIGURE 8: Glucose set point and hormonal balance]**
A horizontal number line showing blood glucose concentration from 40 mg/dL (hypoglycemic danger zone, red) through 70–100 mg/dL (normal fasting, green) to 140+ mg/dL (hyperglycemic danger zone, red). Above the line: "Insulin↑ (fed state)" with curve down toward normal. Below the line: "Glucagon↑ (fasted state)" with curve up toward normal. The two hormones are shown as balanced forces pushing the set point back to the center. Label: "Neither hormone is in charge; both respond to the same stimulus (blood glucose). The set point oscillates in a narrow range."

**[FIGURE 9: Fed state (after a meal): insulin dominance]**
Timeline or snapshot showing:
- Glucose enters blood from intestine (arrow in, labeled "dietary glucose")
- Blood glucose rises to 120–160 mg/dL (graph showing ↑)
- Pancreas beta cells (shown as cells in islet) detect rise and release insulin (arrow out, labeled "insulin ↑")
- Insulin circulates in blood (represented by multiple insulin molecules)
- Target cells (muscle, liver, fat) with insulin receptors respond:
  - Muscle: "Glucose uptake ↑, glycogen synthesis ↑, fat oxidation ↓"
  - Liver: "Glucose uptake ↑, glycogen synthesis ↑"
  - Fat cells: "Glucose uptake ↑, triglyceride synthesis ↑"
- Result: "Blood glucose falls back to 90–100 mg/dL within 2–3 hours"
- Insulin release is inhibited (feedback)

**[FIGURE 10: Fasted state (between meals): glucagon dominance]**
Timeline or snapshot showing:
- Glucose is no longer entering blood from intestine (no arrow)
- Blood glucose gradually falls (graph showing ↓) toward 70–80 mg/dL
- Pancreas alpha cells (shown as separate cells in islet) detect drop and release glucagon (arrow out, labeled "glucagon ↑")
- Glucagon circulates in blood
- Target cells (mostly liver, weakly adipose) respond:
  - Liver: "Glycogen breakdown (glycogenolysis) ↑, glucose production ↑, glucose release into blood"
  - Adipose: "Lipolysis ↑, fatty acid release into blood"
- Muscle: "Fatty acid uptake ↑, glucose uptake ↓" (sparing glucose for brain)
- Result: "Blood glucose maintained at 70–90 mg/dL steadily for hours"
- Glucagon release is inhibited when glucose rises back (feedback)

**[FIGURE 11: The switch from fed to fasted state]**
A graph showing:
- X-axis: time after meal (0 to 6 hours)
- Y-axis: blood glucose (50–180 mg/dL) and hormone levels (insulin, glucagon)
- Insulin curve: sharp peak at 30 min (~50 mU/mL), then falls exponentially, reaching low levels (~5 mU/mL) by 3 hours
- Glucagon curve: suppressed during first 1 hour (food still arriving), then rises as blood glucose falls, reaching 50–100 pg/mL by 3 hours
- Glucose curve: rises from 90 to 150 mg/dL at 30 min, then falls back, stabilizing at 80–90 mg/dL by 2–3 hours
- Annotations: "Fed state (0–2 hr): insulin high, glucagon low, glucose rises then falls" and "Fasted state (2–6 hr): insulin low, glucagon rising, glucose steady"

**[FIGURE 12: Glucose homeostasis as a seesaw]**
Two children on a seesaw, labeled "Insulin" (left) and "Glucagon" (right), balanced on a fulcrum labeled "Blood glucose set point (~90 mg/dL)." When glucose rises (heavy weight on left), insulin rises and pushes down, lowering glucose back to center. When glucose falls (heavy weight on right), glucagon rises and pushes down, raising glucose back to center. Neither child is permanently higher; they balance around the fulcrum.

**[FIGURE 13: Type 1 vs. Type 2 diabetes (hormonal basis)]**
Two side-by-side bar graphs:
- **Left (Type 1 diabetes):** Shows zero insulin (flat line at baseline), elevated glucagon (tall bar), and elevated glucose (tall bar). Label: "Beta cells destroyed. No insulin. Glucagon rises but cannot lower glucose alone. Result: persistent hyperglycemia."
- **Right (Type 2 diabetes):** Shows normal or high insulin (tall bar), low glucagon (suppressed), but still elevated glucose (tall bar, somewhat higher than normal). Label: "Cells resist insulin. Glucose rises despite insulin presence. Pancreas releases more insulin, but target cells don't respond. Result: insulin resistance + hyperglycemia."

### Supporting detail (for future development or advanced sections)

- **Steroid hormone synthesis pathways:** pregnenolone → three zones of adrenal cortex, each producing different end products (mineralocorticoids, glucocorticoids, androgens)
- **Second messenger cascades:** full detail of cAMP pathway (G protein → adenylyl cyclase → ATP → cAMP → protein kinase → phosphorylation cascade)
- **IP3/calcium signaling:** alternative second messenger system (G protein → phospholipase C → IP3 + DAG → calcium release + protein kinase activation)
- **Menstrual cycle:** FSH/LH pulsatile secretion, follicular phase, ovulation, luteal phase, progesterone/estrogen oscillation
- **Spermatogenesis:** FSH-driven spermatogonium → spermatocyte → spermatid → spermatozoon
- **Growth hormone's metabolic effects:** direct effects (lipolysis) + indirect effects via IGF-1 (protein synthesis, cell growth)
- **Stress response timeline:** SAM pathway (seconds) vs. HPA axis (minutes to hours), general adaptation syndrome (alarm → resistance → exhaustion)

## Image creation notes

These diagrams should be:
- **Clear and labeled:** Every component named; arrows show direction of signal flow.
- **Color-coded:** Water-soluble hormones (blue), lipid-soluble hormones (red/orange), feedback inhibition (black X or ⊣), stimulation (arrow), baseline/normal (green).
- **Scaled for learning:** Show time (seconds vs. hours), organ locations (where is the pituitary?), effects on blood levels (graphs when helpful).
- **Not cluttered:** Avoid overwhelming detail; defer minutiae to pantry.
- **Reusable:** Can be referenced in subsequent chapters on metabolism, stress, reproduction, bone.
